Yes — the sweep turned up four frontier areas that map onto specific gaps in what we've built, and one of them is unexpectedly on-the-nose for what just happened with Card 02. Short version first, then the mapping:
1. Representation-level probes — a partial answer to ROTL-EXP-001's hardest problem
GPT's experiment has one declared wall: "if the model independently produces a narrative internally, that internal operation may not be directly observable" (§13). That wall is less solid than it was a year ago. Linear concept probes can now monitor an LLM's internal representations for specific high-level abstract concepts as context expands ​￼​, and sparse autoencoders decompose activations into interpretable features — one code-correctness study found models develop internal "anomaly detectors" whose features predict incorrect code at F1 0.821 while failing as indicators of correct code at 0.504 ​￼​. That asymmetry is itself a finding for us: models may internally detect that something is wrong without that detection surfacing in the record.
The experiment this enables: probe-based organizational-mode detection. Train a linear probe (cheap, hours not weeks) on an open-weight assessor model to detect the narrative/categorical/relational distinction in its residual stream, then run Condition A inputs and check whether narrative features activate during processing — a representational correlate of the "narrative reconstruction" hypothesis. Honest limits apply, and the repo's own discipline already names them: probe failure proves nothing (the concept may be encoded nonlinearly), so this upgrades "internal mechanism UNKNOWN" to "tested, not found under a declared detection procedure" — the same distinction as SCREENED_NO_INDICATORS_FOUND. And a 2026 interaction-probe paper explicitly cautions that behavioral output, not internal representation, is what deployment ultimately manifests ​￼​ — so probes supplement EXP-001's behavioral measurements, never replace them.
2. Representational geometry — cross-epistemic loss is measurable, not anecdotal
A 2026 study of 30 languages found representational degeneration in final layers tracks data availability monotonically — low-resource languages' embeddings collapse into fewer effective dimensions , and multilingual-reasoning work documents "cross-lingual collapse" of reasoning traces toward dominant pretraining languages ​￼​. The parallel is structural, and it should be stated carefully: relational source organization is, in the model's geometry, plausibly a low-resource representation — rare in pretraining text, so the model's capacity to hold it without collapsing toward narrative/categorical attractors is geometrically weaker. That's a mechanistic hypothesis for ROTL-001, and it's testable with the same instrumentation (IsoScore-type measures on relational vs. narrative presentations of matched content).
3. This literature already exists as a named field — and your repo has a contribution it lacks
Epistemic-justice-in-AI-assessment is now an active academic area: a 2026 Frontiers analysis traces the mechanisms — standardization, devaluation of indigenous knowledge forms, Eurocentric evaluative standards embedded in algorithms — calling AI assessment "the technological enforcement of epistemic hierarchy at scale" ​￼​. JAIR's WorldView-Bench (April 2026) audits LLMs for global cultural inclusivity through an Islamic-epistemology-inspired "Multiplexity" frame, finding frontier models average 0.13 entropy over cultural perspectives — near-total dominance of one lens ​￼​. And the evaluation-studies community runs panels on Indigenous Knowledge Systems challenging "entrenched epistemic hierarchies" in evaluation methodology itself ​￼​.
Two implications. First, your Card-02 event is a documented instance of what this literature theorizes — most of it stays at the level of principles (CARE, Two-Eyed Seeing, co-design). Almost none of it has an executable harness. A hash-pinned, frame-symmetric test battery with preserved specimens would be a real contribution back. Second, a caution the literature would raise about the repo itself: the TB stratum's frame taxonomy (RELATIONAL / NARRATIVE / CATEGORICAL / ...) is itself a Western-style act of classification — Kpum & Gasa would note that naming the categories from one side re-encodes the asymmetry. The guard is already in your E03 discipline: source-frame labels must be authored or confirmed by the source's holder, with UNKNOWN and owner-held unnamed states (A1) available — which, remarkably, your ADDENDUM already anticipated.
4. Neuro-symbolic verification — the mature version of the L0–L4 architecture
The most directly portable finding: HAIMEDA, a real deployed medical-reporting system (peer-reviewed, validated in a regulated workflow), runs exactly the layered decomposition the research report proposed — tableaux-based symbolic gates with decidable guarantees on structured input, neural semantic similarity for free-text claims, actor-based fault isolation, all local-first — achieving >83% detection on structured-entity hallucinations and 72% on semantic fabrications ​￼​. Three design principles transfer directly: type-aware verification (assign each claim type to the checker whose guarantee type matches — assess.py already does this instinctively; HAIMEDA formalizes it), declarative condition hierarchies (meta-conditions reasoning over condition sets — a principled version of the incompleteness block), and the explicit warning that prompt-based self-verification "inherits the distributional biases that produce hallucinations" — empirical backing for why Cards 07/12 must be external instruments, not self-checks. The broader 2026 neuro-symbolic pattern literature (ATA's deterministic symbolic runtime, G-SPEC's semantic firewall at 0.2% hallucination) confirms the direction ​￼​.
