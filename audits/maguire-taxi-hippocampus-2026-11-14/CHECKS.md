# Checks: London taxi-driver hippocampus studies

Co-authored. CC0. This file says nothing about the authors. It addresses the study designs and the reported results.

Short names:
- **M2000**: Maguire et al. 2000, PNAS. Full text read.
- **M2006**: Maguire, Woollett & Spiers 2006, Hippocampus. Abstract only.
- **W2011**: Woollett & Maguire 2011, Current Biology. Main text read; the supplement was not.

## C1. Anomaly: plasticity is mostly additive elsewhere; why a trade-off here?

**OBSERVED**
- M2000 found no difference in total hippocampal volume, only in how it was distributed (E1.5, E2.1). The anterior difference did not survive correction in VBM and was confirmed by pixel counting (E5.1).
- In M2000, anterior volume fell with time as a taxi driver (r = −0.6, E2.2). That time counts training and working years together (E1.7).
- W2011 is longitudinal. The posterior hippocampus grew, no anterior decrease was seen, and nothing changed elsewhere in the brain (E1.16, E2.9).
- W2011's only behavioural cost is worse 30-minute delayed recall of the Taylor complex figure in qualified trainees (E2.8).
- M2006 reports new visuo-spatial learning worse than in bus drivers. Its link to anterior volume is worded "We speculate" (E2.4, E2.5).
- Draganski 2004: findings not fetched (E-context.3). The premise that plasticity elsewhere is "mostly additive" is carried.

**DERIVED**
- The volume trade-off, larger posterior with smaller anterior, appears only in cross-sectional comparisons of licensed drivers (M2000, M2006).
- The one within-person volume measurement (W2011) is additive over 3–4 years: a gain with no measured loss.
- So the anomaly is narrower than posed. What these sources show as zero-sum is two things:
  - (a) a cross-sectional anterior difference that grows with tenure;
  - (b) one memory test in W2011, plus "new visuo-spatial information" in M2006, test names unknown.
- W2011 offers a timing reading, that the anterior loss might come later (E2.9, E2.10). It is labelled "We speculate" and was not measured.
- Tenure as a taxi driver covers years of navigation and years of everything else the job involves. M2000's correlation cannot separate these (E1.7).

**PROPOSED**
- Report anterior volume against the two parts of tenure separately: training years and working years.
- Run W2011's design past qualification. A T3 several years into working life would show whether the anterior decrease appears, and when.

## C2. Navigation as baseline: disuse, not use, as the risk

**OBSERVED**
- Dahmani & Bohbot 2020 (abstract): greater lifetime GPS use goes with worse spatial memory during self-guided navigation. In a small longitudinal subsample, more GPS use went with a steeper decline (E-context.1, E-context.2).
- W2011 cites, but this audit did not read, a retired-driver study. In it the volume and memory pattern was reported reversed or "normalized" once demand fell (E2.12).
- The evolutionary premise (navigation of new terrain throughout human history) is carried, not tested here.

**DERIVED**
- The deficit these studies report is not a navigation deficit. Taxi drivers did better on London landmarks (E1.14). The cost is on recalling a novel complex figure (E2.8) or acquiring new visuo-spatial information (E2.4).
- "Navigating degrades memory" is therefore not what the sources claim. Their claim is narrower: London-scale spatial memory goes with a cost on one other memory measure.
- The disuse finding (D2020) and these studies do not conflict. They measure different things: self-guided navigation in a virtual task, against figure recall and London knowledge.

**PROPOSED**
- Give taxi drivers, GPS-reliant drivers and non-drivers the same self-guided navigation task used in D2020, alongside the Taylor figure. That separates "use costs another memory" from "disuse costs navigation memory".

## C3. Spatial network: why equate navigation with a hippocampal trade-off?

**OBSERVED**
- M2000 whole-brain VBM found gray-matter differences in the hippocampi only (E1.6). W2011 found no change outside the hippocampus (E1.16).
- M2000 says the hippocampus "does not support navigation in isolation from other brain regions" (E5.3).
- Neither paper names entorhinal, parietal or retrosplenial cortex in the quoted text. Grid cells are not discussed.

**DERIVED**
- A null in whole-brain VBM is a null for gray-matter volume at the stated threshold. It is not evidence that a region is uninvolved functionally.
- Grid cells are a cellular property and cannot be resolved by MRI volume at these voxel sizes. The scans measure the hippocampus's structural signal, not the network's function.
- So the hippocampal focus follows the measure (VBM) and the a priori region of interest (W2011 corrects within the hippocampus). The data do not show navigation to be a hippocampal-only process.

**PROPOSED**
- Add functional or diffusion measures of entorhinal–retrosplenial–parietal connectivity to any repeat. Report network-level results beside the hippocampal ones.

## C4. Grid density: couriers would show it if the London grid were causal

**OBSERVED**
- No source here includes couriers, bike couriers or any other unscripted-route London occupation. The comparison groups were:
  - M2000: non-taxi scan-database controls (E3.1, E3.2);
  - M2006: bus drivers on constrained routes (E3.3);
  - W2011: failed trainees and 31 controls of unstated occupation (E3.5, E3.8).

**DERIVED**
- In this set the hypothesis is untested, not refuted.
- Bus drivers vary route freedom but drive the same grid. So M2006 holds grid density roughly constant and cannot test it.

**PROPOSED**
- Courier arm (below): novel routes in the same grid without the Knowledge examination.

## C5. Responders vs absorbers: acting on emotional load vs absorbing it

**OBSERVED**
- Zero hits for stress, cortisol, passenger, emotion or fare in the full texts of M2000 and W2011 (E4.1, E4.5).
- M2006 says bus drivers were "matched for ... levels of stress". How stress was measured is unknown (E3.3, E4.4).
- The responder/absorber distinction, and surface acting for tips, are carried. No source here measures them.

**DERIVED**
- A general stress measure matched between taxi and bus drivers does not test this distinction. Two jobs can carry equal global stress and differ in the specific load:
  - one-on-one disclosure with no means to act;
  - against diffuse passenger contact.
- Whether M2006's measure could separate them depends on the instrument, which is unknown (E4.4).

**PROPOSED**
- Add a control/agency scale and a surface-acting scale. Add responder arms (police, fire, EMS) as a comparison: high load with the ability to act.

## C6. Stress mechanism: glucocorticoids and the anterior hippocampus

**OBSERVED**
- The anterior hippocampus is smaller in long-tenure licensed drivers (M2000, E2.2; M2006, E1.9).
- In W2011, qualified trainees showed the Taylor recall deficit at T2 "just after qualification" (E1.17, E2.8) with no anterior volume decrease (E2.9).
- Stress was not measured in W2011 (E4.5).
- The glucocorticoid literature (Sapolsky, McEwen) and the premise that the anterior hippocampus is the stress/emotion-regulation region are carried, not fetched.

**DERIVED**
- W2011 bears on the proposed link in two ways.
  - First, the memory deficit appears at qualification, before trainees have worked with passengers. Passenger unloading therefore cannot be the whole cause of the deficit in that sample.
  - Second, the region the stress link targets did not shrink during training. It is smaller only in drivers with years of working tenure, which is when passenger exposure accumulates.
- The two findings point in different directions: the behavioural deficit precedes passenger work; the anterior volume difference tracks working years.
- A stress-to-anterior-volume link is neither tested nor excluded by these data.
- Stress during training, such as examination pressure, is a separate unmeasured candidate.

**PROPOSED**
- Hair cortisol, a months-long integrated measure, at T1, T2 and a working-life T3. Anterior volume would be modelled on cortisol and on navigation load together.

## C7. Practitioner crossover (single case)

**OBSERVED (report only, n = 1, anecdotal, not verifiable here)**
- A practitioner reports the same person planning novel routes in two jobs. Memory was worse while taxi driving with one-on-one passenger unloading, and normal again as a courier.
- The same practitioner reports that bus driving with passengers did not produce it. The account offered is that unloading on the driver is diffused by other riders and by norms.

**DERIVED**
- A within-person switch holds trait factors constant. That is the strength of the report.
- It is confounded with order, age, time and life events, and memory was self-assessed.
- The recovery after leaving taxi work is consistent with the retired-driver normalisation W2011 cites (E2.12). That study concerns stopping driving altogether, so it cannot separate stopping navigation from stopping passenger work.
- The report predicts that the courier arm would show no deficit. W2011's pre-passenger deficit (C6) predicts that a deficit could appear from route learning alone. The study below can tell these apart.

## C8. Selection: does W2011's baseline rule out pre-existing differences?

**OBSERVED**
- At T1 there were no group differences in memory, IQ or background measures (E-selection.1). There were no gray-matter differences, even at an uncorrected p < 0.005 (E-selection.2).
- Twenty trainees did not return at T2 (E-selection.3).
- Qualified trainees trained twice as many hours per week (E3.6).
- The authors raise a genetic predisposition toward plasticity as possible (E5.8). They note that the reasons trainees gave for stopping "may have masked" difficulties with spatial memory (E5.7).

**DERIVED**
- The baseline rules out detectable pre-existing differences at T1 on the measured variables. A null at a threshold is not equivalence: power is not reported in the main text.
- It does not rule out differences in plasticity, in training dose, or in who stayed (attrition).
- Because qualified trainees trained twice the hours per week, qualifying is confounded with training dose. "Failed trainees saw the same milieu" (E3.7) holds for setting, not for dose.
- M2000 and M2006 are cross-sectional and have no baseline.

**PROPOSED**
- Report the posterior change against hours of training as a continuous predictor, within the qualified and failed groups. Report attrition characteristics at T1 for those who did not return.

## Proposed study

Each arm isolates one factor; together they separate the candidate causes. All measures are taken at entry and at intervals after.

| Arm | One-on-one unloading | Novel routes | Passengers diffused | Ability to act on load |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Taxi | yes | yes | no | low |
| Courier (incl. bike) | no | yes | n/a | n/a |
| Bus | no | no (fixed) | yes | low |
| Bartender / hairdresser | yes | no | no | low |
| Counselor | yes (trained) | no | no | trained structure |
| Responder (police, fire, EMS), optional | acute, not unloading | yes | n/a | high |

Measures:
- Hair cortisol.
- Anterior and posterior hippocampal volume, using the same method across arms.
- A memory battery that includes Taylor complex figure delayed recall and London landmarks, plus a self-guided navigation task as in D2020.
- A surface-acting scale.
- A control/agency scale.
- Years in role, split into training and working years.

What each contrast tests:
- **Taxi vs courier:** unloading, with routes held constant.
- **Courier vs bus:** novel routes, with unloading absent in both.
- **Bartender/hairdresser vs taxi:** unloading with and without navigation.
- **Counselor vs bartender/hairdresser:** trained vs untrained unloading.
- **Responder vs taxi:** acting on load vs absorbing it.

Disconfirming results, stated before any run:
- The unloading hypothesis is weakened if couriers show the same anterior and memory pattern as taxi drivers. It is also weakened if bartenders and hairdressers do not differ from couriers on cortisol, anterior volume or memory.
- The navigation-only account is weakened if couriers show no memory deficit and taxi drivers do.

## OPEN QUESTIONS

These need investigation to find out what is actually going on.

1. Across expertise studies plasticity is reported as mostly additive. Is the taxi-driver trade-off real in volume, or only in cross-sectional comparisons and one memory test, given that W2011's longitudinal data showed gain with no measured anterior loss?
2. If navigating new terrain degraded memory it would be species-wide. Is the risk disuse (GPS, D2020) rather than use? Does the reported cost concern navigation at all, or only recall of a novel figure?
3. Spatial processing uses entorhinal grid cells and parietal and retrosplenial cortex. Why is navigation equated with a hippocampal trade-off when the method (VBM) and the a priori region of interest favour the hippocampus?
4. If the central-London grid were causal, couriers and bike couriers in the same grid would show the pattern. Do they? Untested in these studies.
5. Do responders, who act on emotional load, differ from absorbers, who take one-on-one unloading with no ability to act and surface-act for tips? No source here measures passenger interaction, emotional load or surface acting.
6. Is the smaller anterior hippocampus in long-tenure drivers linked to chronic glucocorticoid exposure? If so, why does W2011's memory deficit appear before passenger work, while the anterior decrease does not appear during training?
7. Does the practitioner's crossover replicate in a sample? That would be worse memory during one-on-one taxi work, recovering as a courier, and absent in bus driving.
8. Does W2011's null baseline rule out pre-existing differences, or only detectable ones? How much of the qualified/failed difference is training dose (twice the weekly hours) or attrition rather than qualification?
9. How did M2006 measure the "levels of stress" it matched, and which tests showed the visuo-spatial deficit? (Body not read.)
10. Who were W2011's 31 controls, and what does the full memory battery in Table S1 show? (Supplement not read.)
